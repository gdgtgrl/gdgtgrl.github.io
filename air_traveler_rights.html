✈️ Air Traveler Bill of Rights

The Essential Guide to Your Rights, Responsibilities, and Advocacy

Know Your Fly Rights

Click on the questions below to learn more about your rights and responsibilities as an air traveler based on US Department of Transportation guidelines.

Airlines don't guarantee their schedules. If your flight is canceled, most airlines will rebook you on their first flight to your destination on which space is available, at no additional charge. Contrary to popular belief, for domestic itineraries airlines are not required to compensate passengers whose flights are delayed or canceled. Each airline has its own policies about what it will do for delayed passengers waiting at the airport; there are no federal requirements.

DOT rules require airlines to seek out people who are willing to give up their seats for compensation before bumping anyone involuntarily. Those travelers who don't get to fly are frequently entitled to denied boarding compensation in the form of a check or cash. To be eligible for compensation, you must have a confirmed reservation. However, no compensation is due if the airline arranges substitute transportation which is scheduled to arrive at your destination within one hour of your originally scheduled arrival time.

If your suitcase arrives smashed or torn, the airline will usually pay for repairs. If it can't be fixed, they will negotiate a settlement to pay you its depreciated value. On domestic trips, the airline can invoke a liability ceiling that is regulated by DOT and that is adjusted for inflation every two years. That limit is currently $4,700 per passenger. Should an airline lose, damage, or destroy the wheelchair or other assistive device, the airline must provide compensation in an amount up to the original purchase price of the wheelchair or device.

The Bill of Rights describes the fundamental rights of air travelers with disabilities under the Air Carrier Access Act and its implementing regulation, 14 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Part 382. An airline, including its employees and contractors, may not discriminate against an individual with a disability because of his or her disability. Passengers with disabilities must be provided prompt and timely enplaning and deplaning assistance, upon request, from properly trained airline personnel. Furthermore, airlines must allow assistive devices as carry-ons in the cabin free of charge consistent with safety rules.

Airlines must permit a service dog to accompany a passenger with a disability in the aircraft cabin unless the dog poses a direct threat to the health or safety of others, or the dog causes a significant disruption or misbehaves in the cabin or at an airport gate area. A decision by airline personnel to refuse transportation of a service dog with the passenger must be based on an individualized and objective assessment of the dog that considers the nature of the risk and the likelihood that harm will actually, or continue to, occur.

The 10 Fundamental Rights

Under the Air Carrier Access Act (ACAA), passengers with disabilities are guaranteed specific protections. Click on each right below to learn more about the specific details.

🤝

1. Dignity & Respect

Treated with dignity and respect by airline and airport personnel at all times.

• Protection from discrimination based on disability.

• Personnel must not force you to accept special services (like a wheelchair) if you do not request them.

• Staff must consult with you on the safest and best way to provide assistance.

ℹ️

2. Information Access

Information about services, aircraft capabilities, and potential limitations.

• The right to ask about the physical limitations of the aircraft (e.g., lavatory accessibility, aisle width).

• Advance notice if a specific aircraft cannot accommodate your mobility device.

• Access to properly trained reservation agents who can detail accommodations.

📄

3. Accessible Formats

Critical flight information provided in a format that is accessible to you.

• Primary airline websites must be accessible (WCAG 2.0 AA standard).

• Gate agents must provide prompt access to the same info given to other passengers (e.g., boarding delays, gate changes) upon request for passengers with vision or hearing impairments.

🏢

4. Accessible Facilities

Navigate and utilize accessible airport facilities seamlessly.

• Terminal facilities owned/leased by airlines must be readily accessible.

• Airports must provide designated Service Animal Relief Areas (SARAs) inside the sterile area of terminals.

• Accessible automated kiosks (at least 25% of kiosks must be accessible).

🛄

5. Airport Assistance

Assistance moving through the airport and boarding upon request.

• Prompt wheelchair or guide assistance from terminal curbside to your gate.

• Help making flight connections.

• Staff cannot leave you unattended for more than 30 minutes in a wheelchair or other device.

💺

6. Aircraft Assistance

Moving to and from seats and stowing items on the aircraft.

• Assistance transferring between an aisle chair and your designated seat.

• Help stowing and retrieving personal carry-on items and small assistive devices.

• Note: Staff are not required to provide medical oxygen, help with eating, or help inside the lavatory.

🐕

7. Devices & Animals

Travel with personal assistive devices or approved service animals.

• Assistive devices do not count toward carry-on limits and cannot be charged baggage fees.

• Priority cabin storage for at least one folding manual wheelchair (on aircraft with 100+ seats).

• Right to travel with a recognized service dog in the cabin at no extra charge.

🪑

8. Seating Accommodations

Receive seating that meets your specific disability-related needs.

• Bulkhead seating for individuals traveling with a service animal or needing extra legroom for a fused/immobilized leg.

• Adjoining seats for a required personal care attendant.

• Aisle seats with movable armrests for easy transfer.

✈️

9. Accessible Features

Use accessible features like movable armrests and accessible lavatories.

• Twin-aisle aircraft must have at least one accessible lavatory.

• Aircraft with more than 60 seats and an accessible lavatory must have an onboard wheelchair.

• Half of all aisle seats must have movable armrests.

⚖️

10. Issue Resolution

Prompt resolution of disability issues via a Complaint Resolution Official (CRO).

• Right to speak immediately with a CRO in person or by phone at no cost.

• If you file a written complaint regarding a disability issue, airlines must provide a written response.

• You may file a complaint directly with the DOT if your issue is not resolved.

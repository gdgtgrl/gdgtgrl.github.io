Origins, Identity & The Evidence

Analyzing Alternative Black Identity Movements Through History, Genomics, and Archaeology

Summary of African American Origins

The historical, genetic, and archaeological consensus unequivocally traces the origins of Black Americans to West and West-Central Africa. Between the 16th and 19th centuries, an estimated 12.5 million Africans were forcibly transported across the Atlantic during the Transatlantic Slave Trade.

Despite the brutal severing of familial ties and the stripping of native languages, diverse ethnic groups—including the Wolof, Mandinka, Akan, Igbo, and Kongo—survived the Middle Passage. They forged a new, resilient culture on American soil, laying the economic and cultural bedrock of the United States. They did not "arrive" from the Americas; they built a diaspora despite unimaginable adversity.

The Alternate Identity Movements

Driven by cultural trauma, a desire for sovereign recognition, and widespread internet misinformation, several alternative identity movements have emerged that actively reject the African origin consensus.

🌎

Black Indigeneity & Soulaan

Asserts that Black Americans are autochthonous to the Americas. They claim the slave trade is an educational "myth" and that a "Paper Genocide" reclassified Indigenous peoples as "Negro." The "Soulaan" identity specifically claims "auto-genesis" from American soil (termed "Amorocco"), rejecting "African American" as a colonial misnomer.

🗺

ADOS / FBA

"American Descendants of Slavery" and "Foundational Black Americans" focus on lineage tied strictly to U.S. chattel slavery, often using the 1870 Census as a metric. While driven by a push for reparations, factions aggressively delineate themselves from recent African and Caribbean immigrants, frequently employing anti-immigrant rhetoric.

⚖

Moorish-Americans

Trace their roots to Moroccan or Islamic explorers who allegedly settled the Americas pre-Columbus. They argue the term "Black" is a corporate tag utilized by the 14th Amendment to strip them of their sovereign nationality. They frequently cite the 1836 Treaty of Marrakesh as proof of their separate sovereign status.

🗜

Hebrew Israelites

Trace lineage directly to the biblical 12 Tribes of Israel. They view the Transatlantic Slave Trade as divine punishment prophesied in Deuteronomy 28. This group explicitly rejects African identity, stating that Africans (descendants of Ham) are a distinct, separate race from God's chosen people.

What Do the Records Say?

Proponents of indigenous and Moorish claims argue the logistics of the slave trade were impossible ("Where are the ships?") and rely on the concept of a "Paper Genocide." The historical reality, however, is backed by an unprecedented volume of global data.

The Transatlantic Slave Trade Database

Historians have meticulously cataloged over 36,000 distinct slave ship voyages. These records are corroborated by port authority logs, insurance claims, taxation documents, and shipbuilder records across British, Spanish, Portuguese, and Dutch empires.

The 1870 Census "Brick Wall"

FBA groups cite the 1870 Census—the first to list Black people by first and last name—as proof of domestic origin. Genealogists clarify this "brick wall" exists because prior to 1870, enslaved people were legally classified as property on slave schedules, not because they spontaneously generated in America.

What Does the DNA Say?

Alternative theories routinely dismiss DNA testing. However, revolutionary paleogenomics and massive consumer databases confirm the historical record, proving unbroken lineages to West and Central Africa.

1. Catoctin Furnace (MD)

Ancient DNA from 27 enslaved 18th-century ironworkers was compared to the 23andMe database. Researchers identified 41,799 living relatives in the U.S. and found direct Identity-By-Descent (IBD) links to Wolof, Mandinka, and Kongo populations in Africa.

2. Anson Street (SC)

A community-engaged study of 36 African-descended individuals from late 18th-century Charleston revealed predominantly West and West-Central African genomic ancestry, completely refuting claims that colonial-era Black populations were actually Native Americans.

3. NY African Burial Ground

Analysis of over 400 individuals in lower Manhattan utilized mtDNA. Studies (e.g., Ely et al.) show over 50% of maternal haplotypes in modern African Americans exactly match specific West African ethnic groups, not Indigenous American haplotypes (A, B, C, D, X).

What Does Archaeology Say?

Proponents of Black Indigeneity point to Olmec colossal heads, claiming their phenotypes prove pre-Columbian African settlement. However, true bioarchaeology relies on physical markers in bone and teeth, which reveal a history of enslavement and African birth.

⚠

Debunking Phenotype Myths

Determining race solely from cranial phenotypes on stone statues is inaccurate and historically racist. The Olmec civilization was indigenous Mesoamerican. Claims of "Egyptian pyramids in the Grand Canyon" are verified early 20th-century hoaxes.

🦷

Isotopic & Dental Origins

Strontium and lead isotopes in tooth enamel from the NY African Burial Ground prove many individuals were born in Africa. Furthermore, several remains showed culturally filed teeth—a distinct West African practice not found in Indigenous American cultures.

The Physical Toll of Slavery

Skeletal remains from sites like Catoctin and NYABG physically document chattel slavery, countering claims of a thriving indigenous empire:

  • ■ Schmorl’s Nodes: Deep spinal lesions indicating extreme, heavy lifting from childhood.
  • ■ Enamel Hypoplasia: Lines on teeth revealing severe early childhood malnutrition and physiological stress.
  • ■ Muscle Hypertrophy: Enlarged bone attachment sites caused by relentless forced labor.

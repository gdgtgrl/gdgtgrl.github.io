The Anatomy of a Federal Audit Finding

Under Generally Accepted Government Auditing Standards (GAGAS / Yellow Book) and the GAO Financial Audit Manual (FAM) / FISCAM frameworks, a complete audit finding must clearly articulate five essential elements. Click any element below to explore writing criteria, examples, and common pitfalls.

Structural Logical Alignment Rule

An NFR fails if the 5 elements are disconnected. Use this interactive matrix to test the logical cohesion of your finding:

1. Condition ↔ Criteria Pair

The Condition must directly measure against the specific requirement set in the Criteria. If criteria requires 30-day patching (NIST SI-2), condition must show patches past 30 days.

2. Cause ↔ Recommendation Link

The Recommendation must directly address the Root Cause. If the cause is lack of automated monitoring tools, recommending a policy rewrite will not fix the finding.

3. Condition ↔ Effect Realism

The Effect must reflect realistic impact based on the Condition scale. Avoid sensationalizing minor administrative gaps as catastrophic cyber attacks.

GAO Yellow Book Audit Writing & Review Checklist

Quality control principles based on Generally Accepted Government Auditing Standards (GAGAS 2021/2024 revisions).

1. Objective & Non-Inflammatory Tone

Findings must present facts neutrally without accusatory language. Use passive/objective descriptions of system states rather than attacking individuals.

✗ Bad: "The incompetent system team recklessly failed to patch servers."

✓ Good: "System patches were not applied within the mandated 30-day window for 14 servers."

2. Root Cause vs. Restating Condition

Do not simply restate the Condition as the Cause. Ask "Why did this happen?" multiple times to find governance or operational gaps.

✗ Bad: "The cause was that users did not turn on MFA."

✓ Good: "The cause was lack of technical policy enforcement in Active Directory and missing automated compliance monitoring."

3. Exact Criteria Citation

Vague criteria weaken the finding. Always cite specific section numbers, control identifiers, or policy paragraphs.

✗ Bad: "Security policy requires systems to be safe."

✓ Good: "NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 Control AC-2(1) requires automated notification upon account termination."

4. Actionable & SMART Recommendations

Recommendations must tell management what outcome is needed without dictating exact proprietary tool purchases unless required.

✗ Bad: "Fix security as soon as possible."

✓ Good: "Implement automated access review workflows to ensure quarterly account verification by system owners."

Senior Auditor Pre-Issuance Quality Checklist

  • Is the finding title descriptive, concise, and reflective of the core deficiency?
  • Does the Condition contain specific sample sizes, error rates, or dates tested?
  • Is the Criteria active and applicable to the audit period under review?
  • Does the Cause explain why controls failed (e.g., personnel, policy, monitoring)?
  • Is the Effect realistic and framed appropriately for the system's FIPS 199 impact categorization?
  • Does the Recommendation provide a practical path to full remediation and POA&M closure?
